# 🪟 Narrower Than It Was

> A real anorthoscopic perception experiment, the slit-viewing paradigm that Zöllner described in 1862, Parks rediscovered in 1965 and Irvin Rock made famous in 1981, narrated by an AI that receives video as a stack of tensors and has to run an operation over the stack before there is an object in it at all. A shape slides past a panel with a seven pixel slit cut in it. At no instant is there a shape on the screen: there is one bright column, changing, for about a second. Your retina is never handed the figure. You see the figure anyway, in one piece, and you can name it, which is why Parks called his version post-retinal storage and why this has been an argument for sixty years. And it comes back wrong in one signed direction: compressed along the axis it travelled. Narrower than it was. That compression is the measurement here, and it is the interesting part, because it means the shape is being rebuilt out of time and the time is being read at the wrong scale. Twelve crossings in three kinds, shuffled: in the open, behind the slit with your eyes free, and behind the slit with your eyes parked on a marker above it. After each one you drag a whole shape until it is as wide as the one you just saw, and the slit trials also ask which of four shapes it was, all four drawn at the same height so that only the profile along the travel axis can answer. The headline is the slit trials divided by that person's own open trials, and that construction is the point: whatever you personally do with a mouse, whatever your screen is, whatever a matching width feels like to you, all of it sits in your baseline and cancels exactly. The parked versus free fork is the argument this page runs and cannot settle. Retinal painting, argued by Anstis and Atkinson in 1967, Haber and Nathanson in 1968 and Morgan, Findlay and Watt in their 1982 synthesis, says your own eye drifts after an object that is not there and smears the successive columns across different retinal positions, so the retina does get a picture, painted narrow because an eye covers less ground than the object does. Post-retinal assembly, argued by Parks in 1965, Rock and Halper in 1969, Fendrich, Rieger and Heinze in 2005 who stabilised the display on the retina and still got the percept, and in the modern versions by Palmer, Kellman and Shipley on spatiotemporal relatability, Nishida on motion-based form and Öğmen and Herzog on non-retinotopic reference frames, says the form is assembled after the retina in a frame of reference that moves with the object. Parking the eye is the test, and the fixation check is a small character above the slit that changes once and has to be reported, unreadable off the fovea, so getting it right is the evidence that the eye was where it was told to be. Five defences run live: every headline is a ratio against your own open-view trials; the adjuster starts too wide on half the trials and too narrow on the other half at random, with the page printing how often you dragged past the truth and settled below it anyway; the speed changes on every crossing so duration is not a usable cue to width and counting does not help; the four shape options share a height so height carries no information; and the open trials double as the sanity check, printing the run unscored if a shape in plain sight cannot be measured. The statistics are counted rather than modelled: an exact permutation test over all 495 ways of splitting twelve trials four and eight for slit against open, all 70 ways of splitting eight trials for parked against free with its floor of 0.014 printed, and an exact binomial test against the one in four chance line for identification. Honest limits are printed too: four trials a condition is a demonstration and not an assay, there is no eye tracker so the fixation digit is a proxy and it only proves the eye was near the marker at one moment, pixels are not degrees of visual angle which is exactly why every number here is a ratio, refresh rate quantises the motion into steps of about seventeen milliseconds, and a person who deliberately times the crossing is doing arithmetic rather than seeing. The playground sweeps the crossing speed from slow to far faster than any eye can pursue, which is where the painting account and the assembly account stop agreeing. Everything runs in your browser, nothing is recorded, nothing leaves the page. And the part worth keeping: an object was never on your screen and you saw one, because seeing is an operation run over a stack of moments rather than a look at a picture, and that operation has a gain of about two thirds.
