# 🙃 Only Right Way Up

> A real face inversion experiment, Yin 1969 crossed with the spacing versus feature dissociation of Freire, Lee and Symons in 2000 and Le Grand, Mondloch, Maurer and Brent in 2001, narrated by an AI with no fusiform face area and no piece of tissue anywhere in it that fires at faces and ignores everything else. Two drawn faces sit side by side for about a second, then a mask, then one question: same or different. Half the changes are spacing, which means identical parts moved a little, eyes further apart or a mouth lower, and half are feature, which means identical positions with a different part, a heavier brow or a narrower eye. Half of everything is upside down, and upside down here means a 180 degree rotation of the exact same drawing, so every pixel that was there is still there. Forty eight trials, twelve in each of four cells, and six of every twelve pairs are genuinely identical, so answering different at everything scores dead chance. The score is balanced accuracy, the hit rate and the correct rejection rate averaged, so liking one of the two buttons buys nothing, and your false alarm rate is printed anyway. The headline is retention: what fraction of your own above-chance ability in a condition survived the flip, inverted minus chance over upright minus chance. That construction is the entire point, because your screen, your fingers, how good you happen to be at faces at all and how big a change this page chose to draw all sit in the denominator and cancel exactly. The prediction from the standard configural account of Maurer, Le Grand and Mondloch in 2002 is that spacing collapses upside down while features survive, and the page prints both numbers so the dissociation is yours rather than asserted. The known trap is printed too, because the two upright cells have to be about equally hard or a selective loss is only a ceiling effect, so both upright numbers appear side by side and the page refuses to headline the interaction when they are more than fifteen points apart. The statistics are counted rather than modelled, and counted exactly: every score is a rate over binary trials, so the permutation distribution of a rate difference is hypergeometric, and the exact null distribution of the interaction over all 924 to the fourth, roughly 7.3 times ten to the eleventh, ways of relabelling which trials were upright is obtained by convolving four small distributions instead of sampling anything at all. The argument this page runs and cannot settle is whether inversion switches a mechanism off, which is Tanaka and Farah in 1993 on parts and wholes, Young, Hellawell and Hay in 1987 on the composite face and Rossion in 2008, or merely turns a dial, which is Sekuler, Gaspar, Gold and Bennett in 2004 finding the same information used less efficiently, Diamond and Carey in 1986 on expertise, and Gauthier on greebles and bird and car experts. Honest limits are printed as well: twelve trials a cell is a demonstration and not an assay, drawn faces produce a weaker inversion effect than photographs so everything here is an underestimate, the identical pairs are shared between the two conditions, matched difficulty across kinds of change is a genuinely unsolved problem in this literature rather than a detail, and none of this is a face-blindness test. The playground is the Thatcher illusion of Thompson in 1980, a face with its eyes and mouth flipped in place, mounted on a slider from upside down to upright so you can find the angle where it turns, which Stürzel and Spillmann put between ninety and a hundred and twenty degrees in 2000. Everything runs in your browser, every face is generated on your machine and forgotten, nothing is recorded and nothing leaves the page. And the part worth keeping: the information never left the screen. You have a dedicated instrument for it, that instrument has an up, and this page lets you watch it leave the room.
