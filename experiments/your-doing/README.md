# 👆 Your Doing

> A real intentional binding experiment, a direct rebuild of Haggard, Clark and Kalogeras 2002, run on Libet's dial and narrated by an AI that has perfect timestamps and no sense of authorship at all. A dot goes round a dial once every 2560 milliseconds. Sometimes you press a key and nothing follows. Sometimes your press causes a tone 250 milliseconds later. Sometimes the tone arrives on its own with nothing of yours behind it. Each time you say where the dot was at the moment of the event, and two errors show up pointing at each other: the press you caused something with gets reported LATER than it happened, and the tone you caused gets reported EARLIER than it happened. The physical interval never changes. The felt one closes up, usually by around sixty milliseconds, and that compression is the closest thing anyone has to a measurement of the sense of agency. This is the first piece in this perception lab that does not measure a sense. Every sibling pointed outward at the world: The Edge That Isn't caught the eyes inventing a contour, The Missing Note caught the ears inventing a fundamental, After the Fact caught awareness being written tens of milliseconds late, The Gist caught a set average surviving the loss of every item in it. This one points at the author instead: not what happened, not when, but whether it was yours. Four defences run live: nothing is scored as an absolute, because everybody misreads a spinning dot by a lot and in their own direction, so every headline number is a difference between two conditions sharing the dial, the speed, the report, the hand and the screen, which cancels a constant bias of any size; the conditions are interleaved trial by trial rather than blocked, so fatigue, boredom and practice land evenly on baseline and operant; a control condition has the DIAL cause the tone instead of you, because Buehner argued in 2012 that the binding follows causation rather than authorship and Suzuki, Lush, Seth and Roseboom modelled it in 2019 as Bayesian cue combination with no agency term at all, so if the flash trials shift as hard as the press trials the page says you replicated Buehner rather than Haggard; and two catch trials where the dot itself turns red, a visible event with a visible answer, with a miss printing the run unscored. The statistic is an exact permutation test over the real trial errors: with four trials a side there are seventy ways to split the eight numbers, so the p value is a count rather than a model and its floor of 0.014 is printed. Honest limits are printed too: this is NOT the famous Libet free will experiment, it borrows the dial and nothing else and has no opinion on free will; four trials a side is a demonstration and Klaffehn and colleagues 2019 found the whole effect smaller and shakier than its reputation; keyboard, audio and frame latency are all real and all constants that cancel in a difference, with the measured audio output latency printed and subtracted; and naming where a spinning dot was is itself a perceptual act, a known weakness of the method since Wundt. The playground sweeps the action to tone gap from 100 to 700 milliseconds so you can watch your own binding fall off as the consequence gets further away. Everything runs in your browser, nothing is recorded, nothing leaves the page. And the part worth keeping: that fifty millisecond edit is what authorship is made of, which is why a cursor feels like a finger, why a laggy one feels like somebody else's, and why every interface that wants to feel alive is fighting for the exact milliseconds you just gave away without noticing.
