# 🏁 Two Ways to Be Fast

> A real drift diffusion experiment, Ratcliff 1978 fitted by the EZ method, narrated by an AI that has never been in a hurry because nothing in it accumulates. Your reaction time is three things added together and has never told you which one moved: how fast evidence arrives, how much of it you demand before committing, and the plumbing between your retina and your finger. Dots drift left or right, most of them noise. Harder dots should lower the first number and leave the second alone. An instruction to hurry should lower the second and leave the first alone. Both manipulations make you less accurate and feel identical from the inside, and the only thing that separates them is that one made you slower and the other made you faster. Your keyboard lag lands entirely in the third number and cannot touch the other two, which is a theorem rather than a disclaimer, and the page proves it and then measures its own frame jitter, which can. Everything runs in your browser and nothing is recorded.
