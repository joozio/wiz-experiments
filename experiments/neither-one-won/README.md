# 🔔 Neither One Won

> A real redundant-signals experiment, fitted to your own hands, narrated by an AI with only one channel. Press the key the instant you notice anything: sometimes a flash, sometimes a click, sometimes both. Both together is faster, and that has had a boring explanation since 1962, because two processes running at once finish sooner than one of them does and nothing has to cooperate for that to be true. But the race story is not a story, it is a ceiling. If your response goes as soon as the first of two processes lands, the chance of answering by any given moment cannot exceed the sum of the two single-signal chances, and that holds no matter how the two channels are correlated, including the worst case where they conspire to never be quick on the same trial. Miller wrote that down in 1982 and both sides of it are measurable from the same fingers in the same blocks. People go through it. Your audio output delay is unknown and belongs to only one of the two channels, which sounds fatal, and it cancels exactly, because every click-only time recorded here already contains it and the clock on a both-together trial starts at the same moment. There is no group to average over, so the null distributions are built out of your own trials: click times paired at random with flash times for the independent race, and click times sorted upward against flash times sorted downward for the fastest race arithmetic allows, a coupling whose distribution equals the ceiling exactly. One block swaps the click for a second flash on the other side of the screen, which is the block that could kill the whole thing, because a bigger and more alerting event would break the ceiling there too and pooling across two senses would not. Then the test: your ear beats your eye by forty milliseconds or so, the main blocks are closed, and the last block delays the faster channel by exactly your own difference so the pair arrives together for the first time. Before it opens the race names the fifteenth percentile of the coming block to the millisecond with no free parameters at all. And the most important section says what the measurement cannot do: the inequality assumes each channel behaves in company the way it behaves alone, that assumption is invisible in the data, and no number of trials fixes it. Everything runs in your browser, nothing is recorded, nothing leaves the page.
